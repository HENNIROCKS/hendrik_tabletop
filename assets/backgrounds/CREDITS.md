# Backgrounds

## bg-fcw1.jpg

Cropped and toned from: *Two mercenary soldiers approach a seated prostitute
while Death sits in a tree, pointing to an hourglass.* Etching after Urs Graf,
1524. Wellcome Collection, Public Domain Mark.
<https://wellcomecollection.org/works/nuac38hn>

The catalogue calls the sheet an etching after Urs Graf, yet its own reference
note cites Bartsch's description of a woodcut by Graf. The Cleveland Museum of
Art holds the same composition as a woodcut: *Mercenaries and a Woman with
Death in a Tree*, 1524, Urs Graf the Elder, accession 1942.118, CC0.
<https://clevelandart.org/art/1942.118>
Whether the Wellcome sheet is Graf's own impression or a copy after it is not
settled there, so the posts call it a print after Urs Graf. The readings the
posts give (a Swiss and a German Landsknecht, the transience of life, the
theme recurring in Graf's work) are Giulia Bartrum's, *German Renaissance
prints 1490-1550* (London: British Museum, 1995), no. 225, which the Wellcome
entry quotes. That Graf was a soldier himself is in both entries.

Details of the same sheet are in the graphics of the post
`posts/2026/2026-11-13-background-image/`.
